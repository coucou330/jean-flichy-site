Used cooking oil (UCO) has become a key feedstock for European biodiesel, and the press was starting to report allegations of fraud. In my first research internship I drafted a market overview for Prof. Olivier Massol. It covers the market structure, Europe–China trade flows, the EU Renewable Energy Directives and the fraud risks, and it was written to support a paper on price links between used cooking oil and the biodiesel made from it.

## Context

Used cooking oil is collected from restaurants and households and reprocessed, mostly into biodiesel. EU rules favour fuels made from waste, so demand has grown fast, and imports from Asia have raised suspicions that virgin vegetable oil is being sold as "used". Very little academic work existed on this market. The Chair wanted a first overview before starting a larger project.
<!-- src : rapport CEC (contexte, fraude, peu de recherche) -->

## What I did

I structured the market with the Structure–Conduct–Performance framework, from collection to biodiesel, with a focus on trade flows between Europe and China.

The most reliable sources available were the three EU Renewable Energy Directives (2009, 2018 and 2023), so I read and summarised them, including the Annex IX feedstock list, double counting and the 1.7% cap on these feedstocks. Comparing import and export statistics then showed discrepancies consistent with large quantities of fraudulent UCO entering Europe.

I gathered about 25 sources into a fact sheet that links every key figure to its source. I also handed the Chair the datasets and figures I had extracted, such as a map of the value of UCO imports by EU country.

## Results

My work produced a draft market overview and policy brief, with all its sources, written to support my supervisor's paper on price links between UCO and UCO-based biodiesel.

That paper was released as a Climate Economics Chair working paper in May 2025, with an acknowledgement of my research assistance: E. Civel, O. Massol and A. Thomas, [*Unfair Trade in the Circular Economy? Price Dynamics in Chinese and European Waste to Biofuel Industries*](https://www.chaireeconomieduclimat.org/en/publications-en/unfair-trade-in-the-circular-economy-price-dynamics-in-chinese-and-european-waste-to-biofuel-industries/), Working Paper 2025-12. My market description supported its context; the analysis is the authors' own.

I also proposed a follow-up research question: the optimal allocation of used cooking oil between its uses and players.

## What I learned

Five weeks were not enough to finish the policy brief, and I know why. I kept searching for more sources when I should have chosen the reliable ones early and started writing. It was a useful lesson in scoping. Lunches with the Chair's PhD students also changed how I see economics: regulation and markets decide energy questions as much as engineering does. Those lunches confirmed that I prefer applied work whose impact I can see.
