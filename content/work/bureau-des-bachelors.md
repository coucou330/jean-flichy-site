**TL;DR** — In 2023 I was in the first cohort of CentraleSupélec's new Bachelor programmes, which had no student association of their own. With classmates from both programmes I co-founded the Bureau des Bachelors and chaired it until February 2025. With a team of 17 and a EUR 40k budget, we organised the first edition of every Bachelor event for 240 students: Welcome Weeks of a dozen events over two weeks, a gala and a first ski trip. We wrote the statutes, the internal rules and a handover guide, and the association still runs with new teams.

## Context

CentraleSupélec launched two English-taught Bachelor programmes in 2023: Global Engineering, with McGill, and AIDAMS, with ESSEC. Students in the two programmes rarely share classes, and the school's student life was built around its engineering cycle, with associations, traditions and a calendar that did not plan for us. Many of us were international students and some were under 18.

A first group presented the Bureau des Bachelors project in October 2023: around twenty students, with roles, statutes and internal rules to draft. That first year taught us more about what does not work than about what does: a few active members carried everything, and the association never really took off.

## What I did

- **Rebuilt the team around clear roles.** In 2024 we stood as a list of ten students from both programmes, with an open letter to all Bachelors: who does what, what we would organise, and a promise that any student could use the association to run their own project, as long as it was legal and budgeted. As President, I was the legal representative, coordinated the bureau and handled relations with the other student associations on campus. The bureau later counted 17 members.
- **Wrote the rules.** With the team I wrote the statutes, and I wrote the internal rules, so that the association could hold elections and outlive its founders.
- **Ran the first Welcome Weeks (September 2024).** Two weeks of events for incoming Bachelors, from a membership drive to a closing ceremony. Each event went through the campus services with a project file and a site plan, plus a rain plan B for outdoor events. For the largest ones we added a safety file, a contracted first-aid team, a temporary drinks licence, a participant charter and parental consent for minors, collected online on a platform our IT team built.
- **Worked with the administration.** In October 2024 we presented our feedback on the Welcome Weeks, the association's structure, the upcoming elections and our plans to the programme management. We asked for the February holidays of the two programmes to be aligned so that both could join the ski trip, and they were.
- **Organised the first Bachelor ski trip.** Four days in Risoul, in the French Alps, in February 2025. We compared four tour operators, negotiated the quote and built the budget around a worst case of 30 empty seats; participants signed a commitment before we signed the contract, and we applied for school and student-life funding to lower the price.
- **Handed over.** With the team, I wrote a handover guide for the next bureau: how student life at CentraleSupélec is organised, where funding comes from, who to ask for what, how to run an event from A to Z, and detailed notes on the Welcome Weeks and the ski trip.

## Results

- **The first edition of every Bachelor event** for 240 students: Welcome Weeks, gala and ski trip, with a team of 17 and a EUR 40k budget.
- **Two programmes brought together.** The ski trip mixed Global Engineering and AIDAMS students, who otherwise rarely meet, after the school aligned their holidays.
- **An association that outlived us.** The statutes, internal rules and handover guide are still in use; new teams kept organising the Welcome Weeks, including in 2026.

## What I learned

- **Structure first.** Our first year showed that enthusiasm does not replace clear roles: once each person had a named job and had been elected for it, things got done.
- **Start early and put it in writing.** The administration and the campus services trusted us because every large event arrived with a complete file, weeks in advance. The Welcome Weeks took us from May to the end of August to prepare.
- **Budget for the worst case.** Planning the ski trip around empty seats and signed commitments let a student association take on a contract it could not otherwise have afforded to lose.
