<p class="intro">I am a final-year student in the Bachelor of Global Engineering, a dual degree between CentraleSupélec and McGill University, specialising in electrical engineering. I belong to the programme's first cohort and co-lead McGill Robotics, and I am looking for a master's programme in energy systems from September 2027.</p>

I like problems where the physics, the schedule and the people all matter at once. So far that has meant modelling Québec's transmission grid, coordinating trades on an offshore converter station, testing weather forecasts for a cargo airship and designing a sailboat that steers itself. I learn best on concrete projects, close to the people who will use the result.

## Why energy {#why-energy}

One night in summer 2026, sixty nautical miles off the Spanish coast, the battery alarm woke our crew of four. The engine would not start and there was water in the bilge. My first decision was to cut all the power. I then spent the night between panels with a multimeter. I found the leak, isolated the swollen engine battery and rerouted the starter onto the house bank, and by morning the engine ran and the lights were back.
<!-- src : lettre ETH ; publication validée par Jean le 22/11 -->

[[figure:night-batteries]]

That night stayed with me because it was a diagnosis and a decision at the same time, taken before all the facts were in. My interest in energy had started more simply. A power cut at home made me realise how much I take electricity for granted. Then I read Jean-Marc Jancovici and the Shift Project's plan for transforming the French economy, and saw that the energy transition is a question of orders of magnitude. I wanted to understand those numbers well enough to argue with them. Living on a boat or in the wild for a few weeks each year keeps reminding me that heat, water and power are finite.
<!-- src : « Personnal statement.md » (coupure, Jancovici, PTEF, bateau/nature) -->
<!-- ⚠️ « Réseau électrique que j'avais fait aux scouts » : à raconter en 1 phrase si Jean donne les détails -->

I have found the same pattern of diagnosing, then deciding, everywhere I have worked. At [Flying Whales](/work/airship-weather-operability/), forecast errors only mattered once they became a go/no-go rule. At [Chantiers de l'Atlantique](/work/offshore-hvdc-substation/), schedules and subcontractor choices shaped a 1,250 MW platform as much as its electrical design did. In my [capstone](/work/quebec-grid-resilience/), an extreme Québec winter only becomes useful once it turns into a list of critical corridors.
<!-- src : lettre ETH ; 1,250 MW : rapport EIP -->

I want to become an engineer who can take the decisions that keep energy systems running when the weather turns, and defend them.

## Education {#education}

I have been at **McGill University** in Montréal since September 2025, for Years 3 and 4 of the Bachelor of Global Engineering, in the Electrical stream. I expect to graduate in May 2027. My cumulative GPA is 3.87/4.00, and I am the student representative for the Electrical stream.
<!-- src : CV ETH -->

Before that I spent two years (2023–25) at **CentraleSupélec**, Gif-sur-Yvette, for Years 1 and 2. The teaching there is multidisciplinary engineering in English, with a mathematics and physics core equivalent to the French *classes préparatoires*. I was ranked 3rd of 80 after Year 1, with a GPA of 3.93/4.00 over the two years and 4.00/4.00 in Year 2.
<!-- src : CV ETH ; INVENTAIRE A ; « Academic Record Summary » (16/10/2026, intitulés des cours uniquement — aucune note par cours publiée) ; brief référents (3.93) -->

Those two years covered a lot of ground. In mathematics: three analysis courses, linear algebra, topology and functional analysis, and probability. In physics and engineering: classical mechanics, electric circuits, thermodynamics, physics of waves, two electromagnetism courses (conduction, then waves), and automation and control. In computing: programming, algorithms, information systems, and advanced programming and machine learning.

There was also chemistry and biology, from atomistics and electrochemistry to cell biology, genetics, bioengineering and ecosystems. The humanities and management side included philosophy and ethics, international sustainable development, modern geopolitics, economics of corporations and two project management courses, plus a modern language every term.

## Leadership and representation {#leadership}

### McGill Robotics

I have been a member since 2025 and Team Captain since 2026. I co-lead a 220-student team that builds three robots: a drone, a Mars rover and an autonomous underwater vehicle. We manage a CAD 100k budget, balanced for the first time after a CAD 30k deficit, and have raised more than CAD 25,000 so far this year. I also lead the hydrophone sub-team. [Read the full project page](/work/mcgill-robotics/).

### Bureau des Bachelors, CentraleSupélec

I co-founded the association and was its President from November 2023 to February 2025. As the first cohort, we had no student association of our own. With a team of 17 we wrote the statutes, managed a EUR 40k budget for 240 students and organised the first edition of every Bachelor event: two Welcome Weeks with a dozen events, a gala, and a first ski trip, for which the school aligned the holidays of its two Bachelor programmes. I also wrote internal rules and a handover guide, and the association still runs today with new committees. [Read the full project page](/work/bureau-des-bachelors/).
