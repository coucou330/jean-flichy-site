  • CentraleSupélec · Year 1 entrepreneurship project · team of five · three months · 2024

    C-Blade — wing sails for cargo ships

    A start-up simulation on decarbonising shipping with rigid wing sails. We compared rigid, soft, rotor and kite sails, scraped data on more than 4,000 ships built since 2015 to pick a target segment, designed a foldable 1,000 m² sail on a rotating base and built a first business case on EU carbon pricing.

    • Shipping

  • McGill · WCOM 206 business proposal · 2025

    Keeping a desert solar park clean

    For a fictional energy consultancy, we proposed an electrostatic dust-removal system for the Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park in Pakistan's Cholistan desert (100 MW, about 400,000 panels). I wrote the literature review on how dust cuts panel output and worked out the power supply — one 3 kV supply per combiner box, about 0.75 W per panel.

    • Solar

  • McGill · INTG 215 · team of six · CTO and CFO · 2025

    SmartBike — a navigation lamp for cyclists

    A start-up case for a bike lamp that also projects navigation arrows onto the road with a laser, so cyclists can keep their phone in their pocket. As CTO and CFO, I wrote the technical plan — a 55-week roadmap from prototype to a pilot batch — and the budget, a four-year financial plan (CAD 20 unit cost, CAD 30 wholesale price).

  • CentraleSupélec Modelling Week · two weeks · 2024

    VizAdrift — could a boat drift home to France?

    We studied North Atlantic weather regimes in historical data, coded a low-order atmospheric model (Charney–DeVore) in Python, measured its predictability with Lyapunov exponents and turned it into winds pushing a kite-towed boat from Montréal. Under a stable blocking regime it never reaches France; with westerly winds it can, in 35 to 100 days.

    • Modelling
    • Weather

  • CentraleSupélec Modelling Week · team of three · two weeks · 2024–25

    Radar modelling with Thales

    Electronic beam steering for a coastal radar. We modelled a phased array, computed the phase shifts needed to sweep ±45° in azimuth, and cut the array to 24 emitters over 4 m — a full sweep in 0.036 s. A Hann window on the emitters limits false echoes from strong reflectors off to the side. Visiting Thales's radar site in Limours made me choose electrical engineering.

    • Electromagnetism
