  • 2024

    C-Blade: wing sails for cargo ships

    CentraleSupélec · Year 1 entrepreneurship project · team of five · three months

    A start-up simulation on cutting the emissions of shipping with rigid wing sails. We compared rigid, soft, rotor and kite sails and scraped data on more than 4,000 ships built since 2015 to pick a target segment. Then we designed a foldable 1,000 m² sail on a rotating base and wrote a first business case based on EU carbon pricing.

    Shipping / Business plan

  • 2025

    Keeping a desert solar park clean

    McGill · WCOM 206 business proposal

    For a fictional energy consultancy, we proposed an electrostatic dust-removal system for the Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park in Pakistan's Cholistan desert (100 MW, about 400,000 panels). I wrote the literature review on how dust cuts panel output, and I worked out the power supply. We settled on one 3 kV supply per combiner box, about 0.75 W per panel.

    Solar / Technical writing

  • 2025

    SmartBike: a navigation lamp for cyclists

    McGill · INTG 215 · team of six · CTO and CFO

    A start-up case for a bike lamp that also projects navigation arrows onto the road with a laser, so cyclists can keep their phone in their pocket. I was CTO and CFO. I wrote the technical plan, a 55-week roadmap from prototype to a pilot batch, and the budget, a four-year financial plan with a CAD 20 unit cost and a CAD 30 wholesale price.

    Entrepreneurship / Product

  • 2024

    VizAdrift: could a boat drift home to France?

    CentraleSupélec project week · Year 1 Modelling Week · two weeks

    We studied North Atlantic weather regimes in historical data and coded a low-order atmospheric model (Charney–DeVore) in Python. We measured its predictability with Lyapunov exponents, then used its winds to push a kite-towed boat from Montréal. Under a stable blocking regime the boat never reaches France. With westerly winds it can, in 35 to 100 days.

    Modelling / Weather

  • 2024–25

    Radar modelling with Thales

    CentraleSupélec project week · Data & Modelling Week · team of three · two weeks

    Electronic beam steering for a coastal radar. We modelled a phased array and computed the phase shifts needed to sweep ±45° in azimuth. We cut the array down to 24 emitters over 4 m, which sweeps the full sector in 0.036 s, and put a Hann window on the emitters to limit false echoes from strong reflectors off to the side. Visiting Thales's radar site in Limours is what made me choose electrical engineering.

    Electromagnetism / Simulation
