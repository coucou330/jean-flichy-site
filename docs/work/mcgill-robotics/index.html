McGill Robotics is a student design team of 220 members that builds three competition robots. As Team Captain I co-lead it with a co-director. Together we approve budgets, chair the executive meetings, organise team-wide events and run the business team. We manage a CAD 100k budget, balanced this year for the first time after a CAD 30k deficit, and we have already raised more than CAD 25,000 this year. I also lead the hydrophone sub-team. I designed its filter shield when I was a member, and a PCB of it is now in testing.

Context

The club runs three projects, each aimed at an international competition: an autonomous underwater vehicle (RoboSub), a Mars rover (Canadian International Rover Challenge) and a hybrid VTOL drone (Student Unmanned Aerial Systems competition). It also organises RoboHacks, a 24-hour robotics hackathon for CEGEP and undergraduate students, and outreach in schools.

The teams had done well before my term. They took first place for the design presentation at RoboSub 2025, first place in the science task at the Canadian International Rover Challenge 2025 (11th overall), and the Dawn Jaeger Tenacity Award at SUAS 2024. Those results belong to the members of those years.

What I do as Team Captain

My co-director and I run the club: we approve the budgets, chair the executive meetings and coordinate the three project teams. We also organise the events that involve the whole team, from design reviews, networking events and orientation day to recruitment events and fundraisers.

I lead the business team, which handles partnerships, grant applications and communication. This year we have already raised more than CAD 25,000. The money goes into a CAD 100k budget made of grants and sponsorship, and after a CAD 30k deficit this is the club's first balanced year.

The rest of the job is representation and practical work:

  • representing the club to the faculty, our partners and our main sponsors, whom we meet regularly;
  • practical matters such as merchandise orders;
  • outreach, with robotics workshops for 13-year-olds and talks in Montréal CEGEPs.

The hydrophone filter shield, from member to lead

The underwater vehicle locates an acoustic pinger, which emits between 25 and 40 kHz, by comparing the signals of several hydrophones. Before those signals can be processed they have to be amplified, and everything outside that band has to be filtered out. I designed that filtering stage.

The circuit is a preamplifier, four band-pass filter stages and an output amplifier, built around op-amps. In my LTspice model the chain gives about 60 dB of gain across the 25–40 kHz band, while a 1 kHz signal comes out more than 100 dB lower. I also checked the design in MATLAB.

This year, as hydrophone lead, I supervise four new members. We have worked through the problem, designed a PCB for the shield and are now testing it.

Results

  • RoboSub 2026: 6th of 68 teams and 2nd in North America. This is a team result, from my year as a member.
  • The club's first balanced year after a CAD 30k deficit, and more than CAD 25,000 raised so far this year.
  • A hydrophone filter design validated in simulation, now on a PCB in testing, with a sub-team of four new members.
