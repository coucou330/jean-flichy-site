WorkProject 07 of 072023–25

Bureau des Bachelors

Building the first student association of CentraleSupélec's Bachelor programmes

Dates
November 2023 – February 2025
Where
CentraleSupélec, Gif-sur-Yvette
Team
17 students from two Bachelor programmes
My role
Co-founder and President
Subjects
Leadership / Events / Budget

In 2023 I was in the first cohort of CentraleSupélec's new Bachelor programmes, which had no student association of their own. With classmates from both programmes I co-founded the Bureau des Bachelors and chaired it until February 2025. A team of 17 with a EUR 40k budget, we organised the first edition of every Bachelor event for 240 students: Welcome Weeks of a dozen events over two weeks, a gala and a first ski trip. We also wrote the statutes, the internal rules and a handover guide, and the association still runs with new teams.

Context

CentraleSupélec launched two English-taught Bachelor programmes in 2023: Global Engineering, with McGill, and AIDAMS, with ESSEC. Students in the two programmes rarely share classes. The school's student life was built around its engineering cycle, and its associations, traditions and calendar did not plan for us. Many of us were international students and some were under 18.

A first group presented the Bureau des Bachelors project in October 2023: around twenty students, with roles, statutes and internal rules to draft. That first year mostly taught us what does not work. A few active members carried everything, and the association never really took off.

What I did

A team with clear roles

In 2024 we stood as a list of ten students from both programmes and sent an open letter to all Bachelors. It said who does what and what we would organise, and it promised that any student could use the association to run their own project, as long as it was legal and budgeted. As President, I was the legal representative, coordinated the bureau and handled relations with the other student associations on campus. The bureau later counted 17 members.

With the team I wrote the statutes, and I wrote the internal rules, so that the association could hold elections and outlive its founders.

The first Welcome Weeks (September 2024)

Two weeks of events for incoming Bachelors, from a membership drive to a closing ceremony. Each event went through the campus services with a project file and a site plan, plus a rain plan B for outdoor events. The largest ones also needed a safety file, a contracted first-aid team, a temporary drinks licence, a participant charter and parental consent for minors, which we collected online on a platform our IT team built.

Working with the administration

In October 2024 we presented our feedback on the Welcome Weeks to the programme management, along with the association's structure, the upcoming elections and our plans. We asked for the February holidays of the two programmes to be aligned so that both could join the ski trip, and they were.

The first Bachelor ski trip

The trip took us to Risoul, in the French Alps, for four days in February 2025. We compared four tour operators, negotiated the quote and built the budget around a worst case of 30 empty seats. Participants signed a commitment before we signed the contract, and we applied for school and student-life funding to lower the price.

Handing over

With the team I wrote a handover guide for the next bureau. It explains how student life at CentraleSupélec is organised, where funding comes from, who to ask for what and how to run an event from start to finish, with detailed notes on the Welcome Weeks and the ski trip.

Results

We ran the first edition of every Bachelor event for 240 students (Welcome Weeks, gala and ski trip) with a team of 17 and a EUR 40k budget. The ski trip mixed Global Engineering and AIDAMS students, who otherwise rarely meet, once the school had aligned their holidays.

The association outlived us. The statutes, internal rules and handover guide are still in use, and new teams kept organising the Welcome Weeks, including in 2026.

What I learned

Our first year showed that enthusiasm does not replace clear roles. Once each person had a named job and had been elected for it, things got done.

The administration and the campus services trusted us because every large event arrived with a complete file, weeks in advance. Preparing the Welcome Weeks took us from May to the end of August.

Budgeting for the worst case mattered too. Planning around empty seats and signed commitments let a student association take on a contract it could not otherwise have afforded to lose.
