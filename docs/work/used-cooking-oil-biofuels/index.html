Used cooking oil and biofuels

Mapping a small, fraud-prone market at the heart of EU biofuel policy

24 June – 26 July 2024 (5 weeks)

TL;DR — Used cooking oil (UCO) has become a key feedstock for European biodiesel, and allegations of fraud were appearing in the press. In my first research internship, I drafted a market overview for Prof. Olivier Massol — market structure, Europe–China trade flows, the EU Renewable Energy Directives and fraud risks — to support a paper on price links between used cooking oil and the biodiesel made from it.

Context

Used cooking oil is collected from restaurants and households and reprocessed, mostly into biodiesel. Because EU rules favour fuels made from waste, demand has grown fast, and imports from Asia have raised suspicions that virgin vegetable oil is being sold as "used". Very little academic work existed on this market; the Chair wanted a first overview before starting a larger project.

What I did

  • Structured the market with the Structure–Conduct–Performance framework, from collection to biodiesel, with a focus on trade flows between Europe and China.
  • Read and summarised the three EU Renewable Energy Directives (2009, 2018 and 2023) — the most reliable sources available — including the Annex IX feedstock list, double counting and the 1.7% cap on these feedstocks.
  • Compared import and export statistics, which showed discrepancies consistent with large quantities of fraudulent UCO entering Europe.
  • Built a fact sheet of about 25 sources linking every key figure, and handed the Chair the datasets and figures I had extracted, such as a map of the value of UCO imports by EU country.

Results

  • A draft market overview and policy brief, with all its sources, written to support my supervisor's paper on price links between UCO and UCO-based biodiesel.
  • Publication. That paper was released as a Climate Economics Chair working paper in May 2025, with an acknowledgement of my research assistance: E. Civel, O. Massol and A. Thomas, Unfair Trade in the Circular Economy? Price Dynamics in Chinese and European Waste to Biofuel Industries, Working Paper 2025-12. My market description supported its context; the analysis is the authors' own.
  • A proposed follow-up research question: the optimal allocation of used cooking oil between its uses and players.

What I learned

Five weeks were not enough to finish the policy brief, and I know why: I kept searching for more sources instead of choosing the reliable ones early and writing. It was a useful lesson in scoping. Lunches with the Chair's PhD students also changed how I see economics: energy questions are decided as much by regulation and markets as by engineering. They also confirmed that I prefer applied work whose impact I can see.
