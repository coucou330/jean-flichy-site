Why energy

One night in summer 2026, sixty nautical miles off the Spanish coast, the battery alarm went off on the boat taking us from Marseille to the Canary Islands. The engine would not start and there was water in the bilge, so we cut all the power. I spent the night between panels with a multimeter, found the leak, isolated the dead battery and brought the power back.

Moments like that remind me how much we rely on energy systems we never look at — and that someone has to understand them well enough to decide quickly when they fail. That is why I am specialising in energy: my capstone models how Québec's grid holds up in an ice storm, and my last internship was on a platform that will bring more than a gigawatt of offshore wind onto the French grid.
